How OpenRabbit behaves before and after connections.

The live product should never present placeholder information as if it were yours. Disconnected surfaces use silhouettes and “Ready to connect” states. This page is where illustrative mock scenarios live, clearly labeled as examples.

Rule: live workspace = real connected data only. Mock information belongs here, in training, onboarding, FAQ, and presentations.

Why are some panels empty?

OpenRabbit waits for an authorized source before showing inbox messages, calendar events, CRM records, social analytics, or listing statistics. A silhouette shows what the interface will look like without inventing user data.

What can work before everything is connected?

The OpenRabbit agent can be ready on its own. Google Maps can operate independently as a real searchable map. Other surfaces become progressively richer as Gmail, Calendar, CRM, social, and market/listing providers are connected.

What does “Ready to go” mean?

Agent Activity starts empty because nothing has happened yet. Once the agent runs, connections are authorized, approvals are created, or actions complete, the real activity history appears.

Why keep a demo mode?

Mock scenarios are useful for training, sales demos, onboarding, and explaining workflows. The difference is that they are clearly separated from the operational workspace so nobody confuses an example with live business data.
