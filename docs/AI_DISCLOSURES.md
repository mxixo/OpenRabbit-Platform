# AI Disclosures — Product Draft

> Product/UX draft for counsel review. This is not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal review.

## Short onboarding disclosure
**OpenRabbit uses AI to analyze information and perform actions on your behalf. AI systems can make mistakes, misunderstand instructions, produce inaccurate information, or behave unexpectedly. When you enable automation, OpenRabbit may act automatically within the connected services and permissions you select. Review important information and configure your permissions appropriately.**

## Automation disclosure
OpenRabbit can perform permitted actions without asking for confirmation each time. Users control connected services, granted capabilities, and autonomy profile. Certain high-risk or out-of-scope actions may still require confirmation or be blocked.

## Unpredictability
AI behavior is probabilistic. Outputs and actions may be incomplete, inaccurate, delayed, unexpected, or inconsistent even when safeguards are operating as designed. OpenRabbit does not guarantee error-free or uninterrupted AI behavior.

## Third-party systems
Actions may depend on third-party providers, APIs, models, networks, and data. Their availability, accuracy, permissions, policies, and behavior may change independently of OpenRabbit.

## User responsibility
Users are responsible for selecting appropriate permissions, reviewing consequential business information when appropriate, protecting their account, and complying with laws, contracts, professional duties, and third-party terms applicable to their use.

## Material actions
OpenRabbit maintains policy controls and auditability, but no technical system eliminates all operational risk. External actions can have real-world consequences and may not always be reversible.

## Trading/financial vertical
OpenRabbit Trading requires a separate financial-risk disclosure and product/legal review. Generic AI disclosures are not sufficient for automated or assisted trading.

## Counsel review checklist
Before public launch, counsel should review Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, AI/automation disclosure, limitation-of-liability language, arbitration/venue if used, third-party-service terms, data retention, consumer privacy obligations, sector-specific duties, and state/country-specific requirements.
